How to Clean an iPhone Speaker Safely

An iPhone has two main places where sound comes out: the earpiece at the top, for calls, and the speaker grille at the bottom edge, next to the charging port, which plays music, videos, and speakerphone calls. Both can collect dust and lint, and when they do, calls become hard to hear and media sounds muffled. Cleaning them is easy, but iPhones need a gentle touch: the grilles are fine mesh, the parts behind them are delicate, and the wrong tool can do real damage.

Where the iPhone Speakers Are

Knowing exactly where the sound comes out helps you clean the right place. The bottom speaker sits behind a row of small holes on the bottom edge, on one side of the charging port. The holes on the other side are for the microphone, not the speaker. The earpiece is the narrow slot at the top of the screen, with its own small speaker for calls. If calls are hard to hear but music sounds fine, the earpiece needs attention.

Before cleaning, check which speaker is affected. Play music to test the bottom speaker, then make a call and listen through the earpiece. Also remove the case, because many cases cover part of the bottom grille, and check that the volume is up and Bluetooth is not sending audio elsewhere.

Safe Tools for an iPhone Speaker

The safest tools are soft and dry. A clean, soft-bristled brush is the best choice: a new soft toothbrush, a small makeup brush, or a brush made for cleaning electronics. The bristles should be soft enough to bend easily, because stiff bristles can scratch the mesh or push debris inside. A small piece of sticky tape can lift dust off the grille without any pressure.

A speaker cleaner tool that plays cleaning tones is also safe and useful for an iPhone. The tones vibrate the diaphragm and shake loose dust a brush cannot reach. Our speaker cleaner tool works in the browser on iPhone, with nothing to install: turn the volume up, hold the phone speaker-down, and run a cycle for 30 to 60 seconds.

Step-by-Step: Cleaning the Bottom Speaker

Work through these steps slowly and gently. Turn off any audio and remove the case, then hold the iPhone with the bottom edge facing down so loosened dust falls out. Brush the speaker holes gently with the soft brush, using short strokes and almost no pressure. Press a small piece of sticky tape lightly over the holes and peel it off to lift surface dust, using a fresh piece each time. Run a cleaning tone cycle with the speaker cleaner tool for 30 to 60 seconds, then play music and listen. If the sound is clearer, you are done; if not, repeat once more.

For the earpiece, use the same approach but be even gentler. Brush the narrow slot very lightly, and use tape to lift dust from the surface. Do not push anything into the slot: the earpiece speaker sits close behind the grille, so it is easy to damage.

What to Avoid on an iPhone

Apple's own guidance is clear: do not use liquids, compressed air, or sharp objects to clean an iPhone speaker. Each of these can cause damage that a simple dust problem would never have caused.

  • No liquids: Do not use water, alcohol, or cleaning sprays in or around the speaker. Liquid can get past the grille, damage the speaker, and affect the water resistance seals.
  • No sharp objects: Never use a pin, needle, SIM ejector tool, or paperclip in the speaker holes. The membrane is just behind the grille, and a poke can tear it.
Warning: Never insert a SIM ejector tool, pin, or needle into your iPhone speaker holes. The membrane sits just behind the grille, and one poke can permanently damage the speaker. If gentle cleaning does not clear the sound, take the iPhone to Apple or an authorised repair shop.

Water in an iPhone Speaker

Recent iPhones are water resistant, but that does not mean the speaker sounds normal while it is wet. If your iPhone got splashed or rained on, water may be sitting in the grille, and the sound will be quiet or distorted until it clears. Hold the phone speaker-down and play a water eject cycle for 30 to 60 seconds, repeat it two or three times, then leave the phone in a dry place for at least an hour. Do not charge the phone while the port is wet, and do not use a hair dryer.

If the iPhone was submerged, in salt water, or in soapy water, drying and eject tones may not be enough. Salt water and soap leave residue that causes corrosion, and the phone should be checked by a repair professional as soon as possible, even if it seems to be working.

When to Get Professional Help

Some iPhone speaker problems are not about dust at all. If the speaker makes no sound, crackles at every volume, or cuts in and out, the speaker or its connection may be damaged, and no amount of cleaning will fix it. Book a check with Apple or a trusted repair shop, who can test the speaker properly and replace it if needed. If your iPhone is still under warranty or AppleCare, check your cover first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a toothbrush to clean my iPhone speaker?

Yes, if it is clean, dry, and soft-bristled. Brush gently across the grille with almost no pressure, and follow up with a cleaning tone. Do not use a hard or worn toothbrush.

Why is my iPhone earpiece quiet but the speaker is fine?

The earpiece is a separate small speaker, and it collects dust and earwax residue in its slot. Clean it gently with a soft brush and tape, and check that the volume is up during calls.

Will cleaning tones damage my iPhone speaker?

No. Short cleaning cycles at maximum volume are within what the speaker is designed to handle. Do not play loud tones continuously for a long time.
