Sound Test

Check your speaker's channels and range with real test tones. Each button plays a different tone through your phone speaker using the Web Audio API. Use headphones or earphones for the left/right channel test — a phone loudspeaker plays in mono, so channels only separate through headphones.

Note on phones: most phones have one loudspeaker, so left and right tones will sound the same through the loudspeaker. That is normal. To test stereo properly, plug in earphones or connect a Bluetooth speaker and run the test again.

How to read the results

  • Left channel: with earphones, the tone should come only from the left ear. If it comes from both or neither, the channel balance or wiring is off.
  • Right channel: the same, mirrored. The tone should come only from the right ear.
  • Stereo: the tone should sound centred and even in both ears.
  • Bass (60 Hz): a deep, smooth hum. Buzzing or rattling at 60 Hz usually means dust on the diaphragm or a loose speaker.
  • Mid (440 Hz): a clear, steady tone. This is the easiest range to judge — any harshness here is a warning sign.
  • Treble (10,000 Hz): a high, thin tone. It will be quieter on a phone speaker, which is normal, but it should not crackle or cut out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do left and right sound the same on my phone?

Because your phone loudspeaker is mono — it plays one channel. Channel separation only exists with earphones, headphones or a stereo Bluetooth speaker. The test is still useful: it tells you the tones are being generated correctly.

The bass test rattles — is my speaker broken?

Not necessarily. Rattling at 60 Hz often means dust or lint is vibrating on the diaphragm. Run the Speaker Cleaner and try again. If the rattle stays after cleaning, the speaker may be damaged — see What Causes Speaker Distortion on a Phone.

Should I test at full volume?

No — 50% is enough for most tests. Full volume can hide small problems and can stress a damaged speaker. Only use 100% briefly if you need to compare loudness.
