Volume Test

Play a real test tone at a volume level you choose and listen to how loud and clear your speaker sounds. This helps you judge whether cleaning worked or your speaker is still quiet or muffled. The tone shown is the tone actually being played — nothing here is simulated.

Not playing

Choose a tone and a level, then press Play Tone.

How to judge the result: at 100% the tone should be loud and clear. If it sounds faint, muffled or buzzy even at 100%, your speaker grille may still be blocked or the speaker may be damaged. Compare the sound before and after cleaning with the Speaker Cleaner.

What the levels mean

  • 25% (gain 0.25): quiet tone. You should still hear it clearly in a quiet room.
  • 50% (gain 0.50): medium tone. A healthy speaker sounds clear and steady.
  • 75% (gain 0.75): loud tone. Listen for buzzing or rattling, which points to loose dust or a damaged speaker.
  • 100% (gain 1.00): full volume. A healthy speaker is loud without distortion. If it crackles at full volume, read How to Fix a Crackling Phone Speaker.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the tone sound quiet at 25%?

At 25% the tone is intentionally quiet — the gain is set to 0.25 of full volume. In a quiet room you should still hear it clearly. If you cannot hear it at all at 50% or above, your speaker may be blocked or damaged.

Can this test measure my speaker in decibels?

No. A browser cannot measure real loudness in decibels without a calibrated microphone. This test plays exact tones at exact gain levels so you can compare by ear before and after cleaning. It does not pretend to give a decibel reading.

The tone sounds different in each ear — is that normal?

This test plays through the loudspeaker, so both ears should hear the same sound. If one side sounds different, run the Sound Test to check the left and right channels.
